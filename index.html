Homework Writing Prompt

"In Chapters 18–20, Harper Lee contrasts the rigid social expectations of Maycomb with genuine human compassion. Analyze how Tom Robinson's testimony and Atticus Finch's closing argument challenge the town's unspoken racial code. (150–300 words)"

Writing Criteria:

  • Length: 150 to 300 words.
  • Focused Thesis: Address how compassion or truth confronts Maycomb's social code.
  • Textual Evidence: Cite specific details from Ch. 18–20 (Tom's disabled left arm, "felt sorry for her," or Atticus's closing speech).
  • Analytical Depth: Explain why the courtroom reacts so strongly to Tom's words and Atticus's appeal.

3 Key Thematic Angles for In-Class Discussion

Discussion Guide
Angle 1 Chapter 18

Mayella's Isolation & The Physical Truth of Tom's Arm

Core Insight: Mayella is both a victim of her father's abuse and a perpetrator of a deadly lie; when Tom stands up, his withered left arm physically proves his innocence while revealing Mayella's desperation.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • Why does Mayella assume Atticus is mocking her when he addresses her politely as "Miss Mayella" and "ma'am"?
  • How do the contradictions between Bob Ewell's and Mayella's testimonies expose the fabrication?
  • Why does Mayella lash out with fury and tears when Atticus asks if her father beat her?
Angle 2 Chapter 19

The Unspoken Code: "I Felt Right Sorry for Her"

Core Insight: In Maycomb's rigid racial hierarchy, Tom Robinson's honest admission that he pitied a white woman violates an unspoken taboo far more shocking to the courtroom than the crime itself.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • Why was Tom trapped in an impossible dilemma when Mayella grabbed him—unable to push her away yet doomed if he stayed?
  • Why does Mr. Gilmer react with outrage when Tom says, "I felt right sorry for her"?
  • How does Dill's emotional reaction to Gilmer's tone highlight pure human empathy vs. learned prejudice?
Angle 3 Chapter 20

Dolphus Raymond's Coca-Cola & Atticus's Appeal to the Courts

Core Insight: While Dolphus Raymond feigns drunkenness to shield himself from town judgment, Atticus removes his coat and speaks plainly to challenge the jury's conscience directly.

Discussion Talking Points:
  • What does Dolphus Raymond mean when he tells Scout that children still cry "about the simple hell people give other people"?
  • Why does Atticus unbutton his vest and remove his coat before delivering his closing argument?
  • How does Atticus frame the courtroom as "the great leveler" where "all men are created equal"?

Exemplar Analytical Response (230 Words): Theme: Compassion vs. Racial Code (Ch. 18–20)

In Chapters 18–20 of To Kill a Mockingbird, Harper Lee exposes how Maycomb's rigid racial code punishes genuine human compassion while protecting falsehoods. During his candid testimony, Tom Robinson demonstrates undeniable physical innocence—his withered left arm could never have inflicted the bruises on the right side of Mayella Ewell's face—yet his true offense in the eyes of the white spectators is moral rather than legal. When Tom admits that he helped Mayella without pay because he "felt right sorry for her," Prosecutor Gilmer reacts with outrage, revealing a social hierarchy in which a Black man's pity for a white woman is deemed an unforgivable breach of racial superiority. In his closing argument, Atticus Finch directly confronts this cynical prejudice. Removing his coat to speak to the jurors as fellow human beings rather than distant authorities, Atticus argues that Mayella falsely accused Tom out of shame for breaking a rigid societal taboo. He challenges the jury to reject the racist assumption that all Black men are immoral and reminds them that the American courtroom is the one institution designed to make every citizen equal. Through Tom's quiet dignity and Atticus's principled appeal, Lee shows that true justice requires individual conscience to triumph over ingrained social prejudice.
